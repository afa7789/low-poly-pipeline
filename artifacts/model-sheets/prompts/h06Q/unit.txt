# redesign: Repackaged inside 1870–1914 as a **tethered observation balloon**, "Well Eye": a small ribbed gasbag with a wicker-and-iron gondola, a hand-cranked winch drum and a steadying tail fin — at 32 px an egg with a box beneath it and a slanting cable, a contour that cannot be mistaken for the rigid airship's long cigar. Captive balloons were the standard artillery observation platform of the period; the winch and cable justify the slow, anchored read of move 375, and the stun becomes a lamp flash from the gondola rather than a dropped bomb. (note: a fixed-wing monoplane was rejected — sustained powered flight only exists from 1903, at the very edge of the window — and a rotor-lifted craft is later still; both would repeat the anachronism this pass removes.) Materials: doped canvas, iron frame, brass winch drum; one emissive, the signal lamp hung from the gondola. Props: ribbed envelope with rigging (separate mesh for sway), wicker-and-iron gondola with a hand carbine, winch drum with a cable running to the ground, chalk board of observer names lashed to the frame. Face occluded: sealed goggles and a filter mask. Pose/anim: slow bob and cable sway on idle, torsional pitch when firing, lamp flash as the stun read. Story: the balloon observers have the shortest life expectancy in the race and the chalk board is rewritten every shift — the sky holds no well, only wind, and no house insures a spotter. Budget: 450–600 tris; 1 atlas; alpha only on the lamp and smoke.
