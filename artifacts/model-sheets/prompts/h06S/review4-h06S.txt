Clean 2D model-sheet blockout on one solid light-grey background (#CCCCCC), drawn as coarse flat design art — a rough flat-colour blockout, not a photo, not a 3D render, not a polished illustration. Five orthographic drawings of the same unit — a Mech Heavy Tank (id h06S) from a dieselpunk dark-fantasy low-poly game — one drawing per panel in a five-panel layout: a top row of three panels (front, left profile, back) and a bottom row of two panels (top-down, bottom-up), with wide empty margins between them. Exactly five drawings, one of each view, no view repeated.

The subject: a short, compact, stubby, chunky cartoon war machine in the style of a Warcraft 3 siege vehicle. This is the main point of the design: the vehicle must be SHORT. Its whole length from nose to rear wall, cannon included, is only about 1.2 times its width and about the same as its height, so that from above it is almost square. No long hull, no long snout, no long barrel, and nothing sticks far out at the front or back.

It is the same vehicle as its predecessor h06T, upgraded, and it must read as that same machine at once. What stays from h06T: the boxy hull with a thick brass frame, the red flank panel, the black deck, two big fat grey wheels per side with brass spokes and a brass band, two copper drums on the deck, and two grey exhaust stacks with soot-black tips in a V. What grows: the hull is taller and wider, the wheels are bigger, the stacks are thicker, the drums are bigger. The small mortar becomes a level cannon in a short, squat turret. The curved grey plough becomes one big bronze beast-head armour mask with three black spikes along its lower edge, set flat and tight against the hull front like a bumper.

Panel 1, top left: front view facing the camera: the wide bronze beast face fills the hull front, with heavy brow, broad flat nose, two glowing blue eyes and three black spikes along the bottom edge. The two fat wheels stand at left and right. Above the face sits the squat turret with the round cannon muzzle facing the camera, and the two stacks rise in a V behind it, with one copper drum showing at each side of the turret base.

Panel 2, top middle: left profile view of the same vehicle, seen exactly from its left side: a short, nearly square side shape. The two big wheels almost touch each other and fill the whole hull length, with the red flank panel in its brass frame above and between them. The bronze beast head is a shallow wedge tight on the front, its blue eye visible, its spikes pointing forward and down, and it overhangs the front wheel only slightly. The copper drum lies on the deck. The squat turret sits at the centre of the deck, and its short level cannon ends no further forward than the beast's nose. The stack rises steeply at the rear, leaning only a little, and stays inside the hull length as far as possible.

Panel 3, top right: back view of the same vehicle, as if driving away from the camera: the square rear wall with a thick brass frame and a grey centre plate fills the space between the two fat wheels. The back of the turret with its blue side plates sits above it, the two stacks rise in a V, and one copper drum shows at each side.

Panel 4, bottom left: top-down view from directly above, with the front at the bottom and the rear at the top as on the h06T sheet: an almost square black deck in a brass frame, with the wide fat wheels at both sides. The round turret is at the centre with its short cannon pointing to the front, and it does not pass the beast's nose. One copper drum lies on each side of the turret, and the two stacks point out in a short V at the rear.

Panel 5, bottom right: bottom-up view from directly below, with the front at the left: an almost square black belly in a brass frame, two grey axles with fat wheels at both ends, one red square block in the middle as on h06T, the underside of the bronze beast jaw as a shallow band at the front edge with its spike tips, and the two stack tips just showing at the rear.

Every panel holds exactly one full-body figure, never cropped, never touching its neighbour. The five figures are the same unit: same proportions, same silhouette, same props in the same places, same pixel height, same ground line. Neutral static pose, the hull level and square on its four wheels, all wheels flat on the ground, the cannon fixed straight ahead and level with no recoil, no smoke clouds; this is a wheeled vehicle with no crew, no torso, no arms and no legs. Squared-on orthographic projection, no perspective, no foreshortening. Drawn with a heavy coarse line over a few large flat blocks, with hard edges and no small hardware: no rivet rows, no chains, no hoses, no gear teeth, no panel lines, one flat tone per surface, no texture, no grunge, no gradients, no gloss, no cast shadows. The whole unit must stay readable at 32x32 pixels, as a short square block with a beast face and two smoking pipes.

Colours, taken from the original icon and the h06T sheet, several clearly different colours: warm bronze-gold beast head and brass frames, black deck and belly, mid-grey wheels and stacks with black tips, strong red flank panels, orange copper drums, deep blue turret side plates, and bright blue glowing eyes as the only light. The black spikes are the only other dark accent. Props are only the ones named here.

Weapon match: this unit attacks at long range with its single turret cannon; the stacks are exhausts, not weapons, and it carries no other gun, turret or launcher.
Rank in its chain: this is the upgraded body of the Mech tank family, visibly bigger and stronger than h06T, but still short and compact.
